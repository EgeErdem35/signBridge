## Introduction

SignBridge-TR (İşaret Köprüsü) is a progressive web application intended to support short, two-way conversations between Turkish Sign Language (TİD) users and Turkish-speaking people who do not know TİD. The project combines computer vision, natural language processing and human–computer interaction within an assistive communication prototype. TİD is treated as a language with its own vocabulary and grammar; recognising a sign label is therefore distinct from converting a sequence of signs into a Turkish sentence. [S1]

The problem addressed is the communication barrier encountered when the two participants do not share a language or an accessible communication channel. The proposal illustrates this through hospital registration, pharmacy, education and everyday service scenarios. These scenarios guide the vocabulary and interface design; they do not establish that all required expressions are supported or that the prototype is suitable for clinical decisions. The intended users include Deaf and hard-of-hearing TİD users, people who communicate in TİD and cannot speak, and hearing non-signers. Blind and low-vision participation is considered in the proposal, but blind-specific independent-use features remain subject to the team/advisor scope decision D-23. [S1]

The product has two planned directions. In Direction A, the application captures a user's isolated signs, derives landmarks on the device, recognises sign classes and maps them to glosses. A gloss is a written label representing a sign. The application converts the gloss sequence into Turkish text, which the user can review and confirm before speech output. In Direction B, Turkish speech is transcribed, or Turkish text is entered directly; the language and rule layer produces a gloss sequence for playback through a prepared 3D signing-avatar animation library, with captions and replay controls. The proposal also includes sign-to-voice, voice-to-sign, two-way conversation and quick-phrase modes. [S1]

The minimum viable product recognises isolated TİD signs separated by user pauses and supports constrained conversion within a selected vocabulary and defined scenarios. Recognition and translation of unrestricted continuous TİD signing are outside its scope. Recognition vocabulary and avatar-clip vocabulary are separate and must be checked against the intended scenarios. The baseline prioritises AUTSL and considers BosphorusSign22k if AUTSL cannot be used under its terms. Supplementary recordings remain a conditional option requiring the necessary scope agreement, ethics approval, consent and data-use arrangements; this section does not authorise collection. [S1]

The baseline uses on-device camera processing and a prepared animation library rather than fully generative signing motion. Raw camera video is not to leave the device; possible external processing of audio, landmarks and text must follow the proposal's data-flow conditions. Technology selection, device support and avatar production feasibility require evidence. The options discussed in the Sprint 3 technical meeting, including YOLO-based recognition and a possible text-only fallback, are exploratory and do not replace the proposal baseline without an explicit scope decision. [S1], [S2]

## Purpose of the Project (Aim of the Product)

The aim is to design, develop and evaluate SignBridge-TR as a real-time, bidirectional communication prototype: TİD signs to Turkish text and speech, and Turkish speech or text to avatar-performed TİD signs. Within its supported vocabulary and scenarios, it is intended to help participants complete short exchanges with less dependence on a third person. Successful independent task completion is an evaluation objective rather than an established outcome. Blind-specific features remain conditional on D-23. [S1]

The development objectives retain the proposal's identifiers so that the SoW acceptance criteria and verification plan can trace back to the same baseline:

- **O1 — Sign recognition:** Recognise isolated TİD signs and assess signer-independent accuracy and browser landmark-processing performance. Dataset benchmark results and live-webcam results must be distinguished.
- **O2 — Gloss-to-Turkish conversion:** Produce grammatical Turkish sentences that preserve the meaning of recognised gloss sequences, assessed on held-out examples.
- **O3 — Speech transcription:** Transcribe Turkish speech and assess word error rate under the defined controlled conditions, reporting other conditions separately.
- **O4 — Turkish-to-sign output:** Convert Turkish input to glosses and play the corresponding prepared avatar animations. Library production, reference conformance and comprehension by suitable TİD users are distinct checks.
- **O5 — Responsiveness:** Measure end-to-end system delay in both directions, with user confirmation and manual interaction time reported separately.
- **O6 — Accessibility:** Evaluate the MVP's accessibility through automated and manual checks. The effect of D-23 on blind-specific flows and associated acceptance criteria must remain explicit.
- **O7 — Usability:** Conduct simulated-condition pre-tests and, if suitable volunteers are available, an additional target-user evaluation. Participant numbers and sampling require advisor agreement. Simulated results do not establish target-user usability.
- **O8 — Privacy:** Verify the specified camera/data-flow boundaries and obtain the required approval and informed consent before participant evaluation. [S1]

The numerical criteria and test conditions remain those of proposal Table 2 and Methods 4–6; this summary does not revise them or report them as achieved. In particular, the existing avatar-library target is not replaced by the number of AUTSL recognition classes. If suitable TİD participants cannot be reached, the avatar user-comprehension measures are reported as not measured rather than inferred from reference-video checks. [S1]

The intended social contribution aligns most directly with **SDG 10, Reduced Inequalities**, particularly target 10.2 on inclusion irrespective of disability: the project seeks to reduce a communication barrier in short service and everyday exchanges. It also aligns indirectly with **SDG 4, Quality Education**, target 4.5, through more accessible exchanges between classmates and educators; **SDG 8, Decent Work and Economic Growth**, target 8.5, through communication that may support workplace participation; and **SDG 3, Good Health and Well-being**, target 3.8, through communication at healthcare service interfaces. These are intended contributions, not measured improvements in education, employment or health. Healthcare examples do not establish clinical safety or replace professional interpretation where required. [S3]–[S6]
