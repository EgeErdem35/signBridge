## Introduction

SignBridge-TR (İşaret Köprüsü) is a progressive web application intended to support short, two-way conversations between Turkish Sign Language (TİD) users and Turkish-speaking people who do not know TİD. The project combines computer vision, natural language processing and human–computer interaction within an assistive communication prototype. TİD is treated as a language with its own vocabulary and grammar; recognising a sign label is therefore distinct from converting a sequence of signs into a Turkish sentence. [S1]

The problem addressed is the communication barrier encountered when the two participants do not share a language or an accessible communication channel. The proposal illustrates this through hospital registration, pharmacy, education and everyday service scenarios. These scenarios guide the vocabulary and interface design; they do not establish that all required expressions are supported or that the prototype is suitable for clinical decisions. The intended users include Deaf and hard-of-hearing TİD users, people who communicate in TİD and cannot speak, and hearing non-signers. Blind and low-vision participation is considered in the proposal, but blind-specific independent-use features are proposed and remain subject to a pending team/advisor scope decision; general accessibility (keyboard, readable text, captions) stays in scope. [S1]

The product has two planned directions. In Direction A, the application captures a user's isolated signs, derives landmarks on the device, recognises sign classes and maps them to glosses. A gloss is a written label representing a sign. The application converts the gloss sequence into Turkish text, which the user can review and confirm before speech output. In Direction B, Turkish speech is transcribed, or Turkish text is entered directly; the language and rule layer produces a gloss sequence for playback through a prepared 3D signing-avatar animation library, with captions and replay controls. The proposal also includes sign-to-voice, voice-to-sign, two-way conversation and quick-phrase modes. [S1]

The minimum viable product recognises isolated TİD signs separated by user pauses and supports constrained conversion within a selected vocabulary and defined scenarios. Recognition and translation of unrestricted continuous TİD signing are outside its scope. Recognition vocabulary and avatar-clip vocabulary are separate and must be checked against the intended scenarios. The baseline prioritises AUTSL and considers BosphorusSign22k if AUTSL cannot be used under its terms. Supplementary recordings remain a conditional option requiring the necessary scope agreement, ethics approval, consent and data-use arrangements; this section does not authorise collection. [S1]

The baseline uses on-device camera processing and a prepared animation library rather than fully generative signing motion. Raw camera video is not to leave the device; possible external processing of audio, landmarks and text must follow the proposal's data-flow conditions. Technology selection, device support and avatar production feasibility require evidence. [S1]

## Purpose of the Project (Aim of the Product)

**Problem addressed.** SignBridge-TR addresses the communication barrier that arises during short, everyday exchanges between Turkish Sign Language (TİD) users and Turkish speakers who do not know TİD. Such barriers can limit access to healthcare, education, work and public services. [S1]

**Aim.** The aim is to design, develop and test SignBridge-TR, a real-time, two-way application that translates Turkish Sign Language into Turkish text and speech, and Turkish speech or text into TİD signs performed by a 3D avatar, so that Deaf, speech-impaired and blind people (blind-specific features are proposed, pending a team/advisor scope decision, and are not an agreed MVP commitment) can hold short conversations without an interpreter. Independent task completion is an evaluation target, not an established outcome. It is evaluated in simulated pre-tests and, if suitable volunteers can be reached, with target users. Under O7, a task counts as completed only if the participant finishes it without researcher assistance. [S1]

**Objectives.** The table summarises the proposal's objectives and success criteria from Table 2 of Project Proposal v1.1 (R6). It keeps the identifiers O1–O8 so that the SoW acceptance criteria and verification plan trace back to the same baseline. Shortened cells do not change a threshold or test condition; the proposal prevails where wording differs. [S1]

| # | Objective | Success criterion (summarised from R6 Table 2) |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Recognise single TİD signs from a phone or computer camera in real time. | MVP vocabulary selected from the Dataset's sign classes (AUTSL: 226; BosphorusSign22k: 744), with priority for health, pharmacy, public services and daily conversation, and fixed after the coverage audit using training and validation data only. ≥85% top-1 and ≥95% top-5 accuracy on the Dataset's signer-independent test split; ≥15 fps of landmark processing in the browser on a mid-range laptop (a laptop target, not evidence for phones). These are benchmark results; live-webcam accuracy is reported separately. |
| O2 | Build grammatical Turkish sentences from recognised gloss sequences. | ≥80% (point estimate, with its 95% interval) of a held-out MVP test set of 100 gloss sequences, separate from the development examples, judged "correct meaning and correct grammar" by two native Turkish speakers with a written rubric; BLEU/chrF reported against reference sentences. The result covers this test set only. |
| O3 | Transcribe Turkish speech in streaming mode. | Planned controlled test: word error rate ≤15% on 200 sentences recorded in a quiet room (audio source not yet chosen; Methods 5). WER for other speakers, devices, distances and background noise is reported separately, without a target. |
| O4 | Translate Turkish into TİD and show it with a 3D avatar. | Animation library of ≥300 signs plus the full Turkish fingerspelling alphabet; ≥75% of signs correctly recognised and ≥70% of 50 test sentences understood by Deaf TİD users. A reference-conformance check against the Current TİD Dictionary video (≥75% of signs passing) is reported separately and does not replace these user measures. Without suitable TİD users, the user measures are reported as not measured and those O4 criteria are not verified (Methods 5). |
| O5 | Work fast enough not to break the flow of a conversation. | Median system time from the end of the last sign (including pause detection) to the start of spoken output ≤2.0 s; median time from the end of speech to the avatar starting to sign ≤2.5 s. Time a user spends confirming a sentence, and manual-mode interaction time, are excluded and reported separately (event definitions in Methods 4). |
| O6 | Make every flow in the MVP accessible. | Compliance with WCAG 2.2 level AA (automated axe-core scan plus manual checks); all four persona flows completed from start to finish with VoiceOver/TalkBack and with the keyboard only. |
| O7 | Evaluate usability in two stages. | (1) Pre-tests under simulated conditions in the defined service scenarios (system behaviour, interface problems); (2) if suitable volunteers can be reached, an additional evaluation with target users: Deaf/hard-of-hearing TİD users, blind users and hearing non-signers. Participant numbers, distribution and sampling are agreed with the advisor before testing. ≥80% task completion (a task counts as completed only without researcher assistance) and mean SUS ≥70, reported per stage and group with actual numbers and limitations. |
| O8 | Protect users' privacy. | Raw camera video never leaves the device (checked by code review and network-traffic inspection); audio, landmarks and text leave it only as set out in the proposal's data-flow table (Methods 6). Order for evaluation participants: ethics committee approval, then written, explicit consent from every participant (collected only after approval), then participant evaluation. |

These are targets set in the proposal, not results. The pending team/advisor scope decision on blind-specific features may affect O6 and O7, so blind-specific flows and the criteria that depend on them remain open until it is made. Simulated pre-test results alone do not verify usability for target users. The avatar-library target is separate from the number of AUTSL recognition classes. [S1]

The intended social contribution aligns most directly with **SDG 10, Reduced Inequalities**, particularly target 10.2 on inclusion irrespective of disability: the project seeks to reduce a communication barrier in short service and everyday exchanges. It also aligns indirectly with **SDG 4, Quality Education**, target 4.5, through more accessible exchanges between classmates and educators; **SDG 8, Decent Work and Economic Growth**, target 8.5, through communication that may support workplace participation; and **SDG 3, Good Health and Well-being**, target 3.8, through communication at healthcare service interfaces. These are intended contributions, not measured improvements in education, employment or health. Healthcare examples do not establish clinical safety or replace professional interpretation where required. [S3]–[S6]
