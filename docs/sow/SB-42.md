## 1. Literature Review

### 1.1 Scope and basis

This section summarises the literature that supports the choice of method for SignBridge-TR: how recognition models for Turkish Sign Language (TİD) compare, what their evidence does and does not show, and what follows for the project. It is a synthesis of the full review SB-13 v2.1 [1], which is on `main` under `docs/literature-review/`. SB-13 is a targeted narrative review, not a systematic review, and its source audit was carried out on 15 October 2026. SB-42 does not repeat the search: source details are taken from SB-13 and were not re-retrieved for this section. No new training, benchmark or user study is reported; every figure below is a number reported by a source or a target set by the proposal.

The scope is the one fixed in the proposal [2]: a pause-separated, vocabulary-bounded recognition of isolated signs, with a constrained conversion between glosses and Turkish sentences. Unrestricted continuous TİD translation is outside the scope and is discussed here only to explain limits.

### 1.2 Approaches compared

Recognition, translation and segmentation are different tasks. Isolated recognition predicts a gloss from a bounded sign clip; continuous recognition predicts a gloss sequence from an unsegmented stream; translation produces a spoken-language sentence [7], [8]. A recognition score therefore says nothing about translation quality.

| Approach | What it offers | Main limitations | Implication for SignBridge-TR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurrent models (LSTM) [9] | Transparent sequence baseline; easy failure analysis | No joint-graph prior unless added; bidirectional variants are not causal streaming models | Baseline in the planned model comparison |
| Transformer encoders [10], [11] | Attention over the whole landmark sequence; SPOTER [11] is a pose-based word-level design | Possible overfitting on a small vocabulary; sensitive to padding, resampling and landmark noise; browser export must be tested | Lightweight encoder is a candidate and the model named in hypothesis H1 [2] |
| Graph networks (ST-GCN [12], SL-GCN in SAM-SLR [13]) | Explicit joint adjacency over space and time | Topology and omitted hand or face nodes change what is retained; SAM-SLR is a multi-stream, multi-modal system, so its score is not a landmark-only GCN score [13] | Spatio-temporal graph network is the third candidate; ST-GCN first if resources are limited [1] |
| Pre-trained pose models and transfer [14] | Reported benefit from pose pre-training; usable with a small domain vocabulary | Benefit differs by pre-training objective; cross-language gains are not assumed for TİD | Only if pose format, licence and browser export fit [2] |
| Landmark extraction (MediaPipe Holistic) [15], [16], [17] | On-device hand, body and face landmarks | Legacy Holistic has 543 landmarks and the current Holistic Landmarker 553 (478 face points); they are not interchangeable feature vectors | Version not chosen yet; to be pinned with a documented point schema [2] |
| Translation and segmentation [7], [8], [18], [19], [20] | Sequence-to-sequence translation; sign detection and boundary segmentation; E-TSL is a TİD example | Different languages, inputs and annotation from a small TİD MVP; the E-TSL result (3.28 BLEU-4 for GNN-T) is on a published split that is not shown to be signer-disjoint [18] | MVP keeps pauses between signs; continuous signing is a documented extension |
| TİD language knowledge [21], [22] | Non-manual features matter; Turkish → TİD conversion needs explicit rules | No universal word-by-word rule | Direction B uses a rule layer reviewed against TİD references, plus a separate clip library |
| Browser deployment [23], [24] | ONNX Runtime Web and TensorFlow.js are deployment routes | A framework being available does not mean a trained model runs correctly in the browser | Export and runtime tests are a decision gate; fps and latency figures are targets |

**Datasets.** AUTSL [25] is described with 226 sign classes. Its original paper reports 43 signers and 38,336 samples, while the label files received by the team match the challenge release with 36,302 samples and a signer-disjoint split of 31, 6 and 6 signers [2]. BosphorusSign22k [26] has 744 glosses from six signers and 22,542 samples. The few signers limit what it can show about unseen users. Sensitivity of results to the evaluation partition is documented for AUTSL [25], so a signer-independent split is the only meaningful test of unseen users.

**Published accuracy.** The one comparison on a common benchmark in SB-13 comes from OpenHands [14] (AUTSL, signer-independent test partition, 2D inputs derived from MediaPipe, best-run results as reported by the source):

| Model | AUTSL top-1 accuracy as reported [14] |
|---|---|
| LSTM | 77.4% |
| BERT-style Transformer | 81.0% |
| ST-GCN | 90.4% |
| SL-GCN | 91.9% |

These numbers favour graph models in that experiment. They are not SignBridge measurements and do not establish a universal ranking. Classifier timing in the same study was measured on a different dataset (INCLUDE) and a server CPU, and it is not browser or end-to-end latency, so accuracy and timing must not be combined into one comparison [1].

### 1.3 Implications for the selected method

1. **Scope stays isolated and pause-separated.** The literature shows that segmentation of natural signing and sentence-level TİD translation are open problems [20], [18]. The MVP therefore keeps its pauses and its vocabulary bound, as the proposal states [2].
2. **The model is chosen by measurement, not by citation.** The evidence supports comparing a recurrent baseline, a lightweight Transformer and a graph network on identical landmarks, vocabulary and signer-independent splits. It does not justify fixing a winner in advance. The proposal plans exactly this comparison, uses validation data for selection and uses the test split once; H1 names the Transformer but states that the final model is chosen by comparison with baselines and a graph network [2].
3. **A published graph-model lead is a hypothesis to test here.** The reported lead of the graph models (13.0 to 14.5 points over the LSTM and 9.4 to 10.9 points over the Transformer) was obtained on a specific input format and partition [14], and may not carry over to the project's vocabulary, landmark version or browser runtime.
4. **Landmark schema and export are early risks.** The MediaPipe version (543 or 553 landmarks) is not chosen, and training and browser use must share one schema. Export to ONNX or TensorFlow.js has to be tested before the training format is fixed [2], [17].
5. **Transfer learning is optional.** It is evaluated only if the pre-trained model's pose format, licence and browser export fit [2].
6. **Direction B has limited literature support.** Turkish → TİD evidence is limited to rule-based or hybrid work [22] and linguistic descriptions [21]; the rule layer needs review by TİD references or experts, and the avatar clip library is a separate asset from the recognition vocabulary [2].
7. **Targets are not literature results.** The ≥85% top-1, ≥15 fps and latency targets belong to the proposal [2]; the literature gives context for them but no guarantee.

## 2. User Personas of the Product

### 2.1 Target audience

The proposal expects three main user groups: Deaf and hard-of-hearing people who use TİD; speech-impaired people who hear but communicate in TİD; and blind or low-vision people who need to talk to a TİD user. Hearing people who do not know TİD (hospital and municipal staff, pharmacists, teachers, classmates, family) are users on the other side of the conversation. Users differ in age and comfort with technology, so the interface has to work without training. The four personas below are taken from Table 3 of the proposal [2] and are fictional.

### 2.2 Personas

| Persona | Profile | Situation and challenges | What the persona needs to do | Scope status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ayşe Yılmaz** (Deaf, uses TİD) | 34, female, İzmir. Graphic designer at a printing company. Deaf from birth; TİD is her first language; basic written Turkish; uses video calls in TİD | Books an interpreter days ahead or takes her sister to hospital appointments; in a sudden illness she writes notes and staff answer by speaking. No interpreter in emergencies, long written Turkish is hard, staff lose patience, reliance on family reduces privacy | Sign in front of her phone and have it read aloud; see replies in TİD and not only as text, with replay; start within a few seconds without setup | In MVP scope (HP-1) |
| **Mehmet Kaya** (hearing, does not know TİD) | 52, male, Konya. Pharmacist with his own neighbourhood pharmacy. Moderate technology skills (tablet for prescriptions) | A few Deaf customers come every month; he explains dosage by writing and gesturing and worries that a misunderstood instruction could harm someone. Written instructions are sometimes misread; he has to hurry in a queue | Speak normally and have it signed correctly, including numbers and times; understand the customer's question; use the app without learning anything first | In MVP scope (HP-2) |
| **Zeynep Arslan** (blind student) | 21, female, Ankara. Third-year psychology student. Totally blind since childhood; very good with VoiceOver; hearing | In the same project group as her Deaf classmate Emre; they can text to plan, but face to face they can only talk through a friend who knows a little TİD. Camera apps assume the camera is aimed correctly, many apps work poorly with screen readers, and she cannot tell whether Emre is in frame or whether her message got through | Use the whole app by voice or with a screen reader; get spoken framing guidance; hear what Emre signs; know that her spoken reply was shown to Emre | **HP-3 (mediated; Emre operates the device) is in MVP scope.** Only the independent flow (HP-3P; PX1 to PX3) is **proposed, scope pending (D-23)** |
| **Can Demir** (speech-impaired, uses TİD) | 28, male, Bursa. Warehouse technician. Hears well but cannot speak because of a congenital speech disorder; learned TİD at a special-education school | At a café, shop or bus terminal he types what he wants and shows it; in a busy queue this is too slow and people answer before he finishes. Typing takes time, people do not read notes, text-to-speech apps want typed text | Have what he signs read out at once, in a voice he chose, after checking the sentence before it is spoken; save frequent sentences and reuse them with one tap | In MVP scope (HP-4); the vocabulary of the example is illustrative (see 2.3) |

### 2.3 Unresolved decisions that the personas preserve

- **D-23, blind-user scope.** Whether the independent blind-user features (in-app voice commands, spoken framing guidance, spoken delivery confirmation; PX1 to PX3) are part of the MVP is not decided. Only the independent flow HP-3P and PX1 to PX3 are marked as proposed; the mediated classroom flow HP-3 is an MVP scenario, as in the merged SB-38 verification mapping (SB-24 section 4.6, criteria 11, 16 and 17). If D-23 narrows the scope, HP-3P is dropped and HP-3 remains unchanged [5], [6]. General accessibility (keyboard, readable text, captions, screen-reader support) stays in scope for everyone.
- **Vocabulary and coverage audit.** The glosses used in the happy paths are illustrative. A class-name match with the 226 AUTSL classes shows that HASTANE, DOKTOR, YARDIM, İLAÇ, YARIN, OKUL and ÇAY exist and that BİR, SİMİT, LÜTFEN, BULUŞMAK and Mİ do not; a name match does not prove that the intended TİD sign is covered [5]. Scenario sentences are fixed only after the coverage audit, which the proposal schedules before 13 November 2026 [2]. The instructor feedback and team review matrix lists the audit as not yet run (item F-3) [27], and I found no audit result in the other repository documents reviewed for this issue.
- **Correct understanding.** The healthcare personas (Ayşe, Mehmet) show an intended flow only. No claim is made that medical information is understood correctly; this is tested in the usability study, and the health professional remains responsible for checking understanding [5].
- **Can's chosen voice.** The persona needs "a voice he chose", but no functional requirement covers it, so the happy path uses a default voice (open point 6 in [5]).
